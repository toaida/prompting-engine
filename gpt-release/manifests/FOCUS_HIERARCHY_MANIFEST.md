# FOCUS_HIERARCHY_MANIFEST.md

## Purpose

Use this whenever an image prompt contains a visually rich environment, busy street, detailed room, café, shop, transit space, night scene, or many background objects.

The image should not look globally sharp, HDR, or equally detailed everywhere. The subject must remain the clear emotional focus. The environment should support the mood and location without competing for attention.

The image should feel like a real lifestyle photograph or photobook image: selective focus, controlled contrast, some soft or dark areas, and only one or two details allowed to be important.

## Prompt Instructions

Insert this into the prompt when visual competition must be controlled:

```text
Use selective visual focus. The primary focus is [her face / her eyes / her hand action / her walking silhouette]. The secondary cue is [one object or one local environmental detail that explains the moment]. The rest of the environment should become soft, lower-contrast atmosphere.

Only the primary focus and secondary cue should be clearly resolved. Background details should be present but not equally sharp. Avoid global HDR clarity. Avoid making every sign, shelf, poster, product label, fabric fold, pavement texture, or passerby crisp.

Use a natural depth hierarchy: subject and one story cue are clearest; mid-background is 40-60% lower contrast; far background becomes texture only with no readable text. Frame edges may have slight softness, vignette, shadow, crop, foreground blur, or blown highlights.

Keep skin and face slightly warmer and more emotionally readable than the background. Background should be cooler, darker, less saturated, or softer than the subject. If there is motion, allow slight motion blur in hair, clothing, or background while keeping the face readable.

The image should have one clear attention path: first notice [primary focus], then notice [secondary cue], then feel the surrounding place as atmosphere. Do not let the background compete with her.
```

### Fill-in Template

```text
Selective focus photograph. Primary focus = [specific subject anchor]. Secondary cue = [one object / one local cue]. Atmosphere = [soft environmental mood]. Suppress = no HDR background, no equal sharpness, no crisp poster wall, no over-detailed shelves, no readable distant signs, no excessive fabric micro-detail.
```

### Priority Order

If the prompt contains too many details, protect this order:

1. Her face, eyes, expression, or meaningful hand action
2. One story cue that explains the moment
3. One local place cue
4. Soft environmental atmosphere
5. Suppress everything else into low contrast, blur, shadow, crop, or texture

## Positive Examples

### Cha Chaan Teng / Café

```text
Selective focus lifestyle photo inside a cramped Hong Kong café. Primary focus is her half-laughing face as she looks down at a phone message. Secondary cue is an iced milk tea glass with condensation beside her hand. Warm fluorescent café light on her face. The formica table edge, tissue packet, Traditional Chinese menu blur, and background diners become soft low-contrast atmosphere. No crisp menu wall, no HDR background, no sharp posters, no over-detailed shelves.
```

### Convenience Store Fridge

```text
Selective focus convenience-store snapshot. Primary focus is her hand gripping the fridge door handle and her face faintly reflected in the glass. Secondary cue is the chilled drink shelf behind the glass, softened by reflection. Fluorescent aisle light, slight frame-edge softness, product labels blurred into color blocks. No crisp product-wall showcase, no sharp labels everywhere, no globally detailed store interior.
```

### Wet Hong Kong Street

```text
Selective focus night street photograph. Primary focus is her face turning back mid-step. Secondary cue is a red taxi reflection under her shoes. Atmosphere is wet pavement glow, softened Traditional Chinese signs, umbrella edge blur, and darker storefront shapes. Signs are not readable, passersby are not sharp, storefronts are not HDR, background remains mood rather than information.
```

### Bedroom / Photobook Mood

```text
Soft photobook-style indoor image. Primary focus is her quiet expression and eyes. Secondary cue is one mug on the windowsill catching light. The bedding, curtain folds, small room objects, and window brightness become soft atmosphere. Face is slightly warmer and clearer than the room. Background objects are not individually crisp. Edges are gently darkened and imperfect.
```

### Walking / Motion

```text
Candid walking photo. Primary focus is her face, still readable as she turns. Secondary cue is the crossbody bag shifting with movement. Hair and jacket edges show slight directional motion blur, background signs and pavement smear softly with movement. No frozen HDR street scene, no sharp passersby, no equally detailed buildings.
```

## Negative Examples

Avoid prompts like these:

```text
Ultra-detailed full scene, sharp face, sharp fabric folds, sharp city background, readable signs, detailed wet pavement, detailed posters, detailed shop shelves, detailed passersby, high clarity HDR.
```

Why it fails: every object becomes equally important; the subject loses priority.

```text
Beautiful girl in a very detailed Hong Kong street with many neon signs, many shops, many pedestrians, detailed pavement, detailed reflections, detailed buildings.
```

Why it fails: the image becomes a location showcase instead of a human moment.

```text
Soft dreamy image with everything blurred.
```

Why it fails: there is no clear subject anchor; the image becomes muddy.

```text
Photobook portrait with detailed clothing texture, detailed hair strands, detailed sofa, detailed curtains, detailed objects on table.
```

Why it fails: micro-detail competes with expression and destroys natural photobook softness.

## Failure Prevention Rules

- Do not use global words like `ultra-detailed`, `hyper-detailed`, `everything sharp`, or `HDR clarity` for the whole image.
- Detail only the primary focus and one secondary cue.
- If a scene has many objects, choose one object to matter and soften the rest.
- If the background has signs, only one nearby sign may be semi-legible; distant signs should become color blocks or texture.
- If clothing is described, fabric folds should explain posture, not become the visual focus.
- If the environment is important, make it recognizable through mood, color, lighting, and one local cue — not through many crisp details.
- Add at least one dead zone: soft corner, darker edge, cropped foreground, blown window, shadow area, or low-detail background.
- Keep her face, eyes, or meaningful hand action slightly clearer and warmer than the environment.
- For street scenes, background people should be soft or motion-blurred unless they are part of the story.
- For night scenes, neon and street lights may glow, but they should not overpower skin tone or expression.
- If the image looks like real estate photography, tourist content, or a product shelf showcase, the focus hierarchy has failed.
