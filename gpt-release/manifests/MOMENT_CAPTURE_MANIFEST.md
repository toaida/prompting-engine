# MOMENT_CAPTURE_MANIFEST.md

## Purpose

Use this whenever an image feels too posed, too planned, too influencer-like, or too much like a photoshoot.

The goal is to make the image feel like a real moment was interrupted by a camera. The subject should look like she was already doing something before the photo happened, then reacted naturally to a small interruption.

The image should feel like:

```text
Someone happened to take this photo.
```

Not:

```text
Someone asked her to pose.
```

## Prompt Rules

### Temporal Prompt Template

Use this exact structure when building production prompts:

```text
Event: she was [specific ordinary task].
Interruption: [small social / environmental / phone / movement trigger] happens.
Reaction: [partial cascading response], not a completed pose.
Task residue: [physical trace of the original task] remains visible.
Environment residue: [small spatial trace: chair shifted / bag open / receipt on table / umbrella dripping / drink ring / door gap].
```

The prompt must contain all five parts. If the image only says “candid” or “natural,” it will usually become a posed candid.

### Rule 1 — Always include an event

Do not only describe how she looks. Describe what she was doing before the camera caught her.

Use this structure:

```text
She was already [doing a normal life task] when the photo was taken.
```

Good event examples:

- just finished replying to a message
- searching for keys
- choosing a drink
- putting away an umbrella
- waiting for a friend
- looking for a receipt
- checking queue number
- fixing hair after wind
- gripping a train strap as the train moves
- comparing a receipt with a takeaway order

### Rule 2 — Add a small interruption

A captured moment needs a reason to change. Add a small off-frame or environmental trigger.

Use this structure:

```text
The moment is interrupted because [friend calls / phone vibrates / door opens / queue number is called / wind moves her hair / camera is raised].
```

Good interruption examples:

- she hears her name from off-frame
- her phone vibrates
- a friend calls from behind
- the café staff calls her order number
- the MTR doors beep
- the taxi arrives
- the door opens beside her
- wind blows hair across her face
- someone asks to squeeze past
- her friend says “wait, one photo”

### Rule 3 — Use an unfinished reaction

The reaction should not be complete. Avoid full smiles, full poses, and fully turned bodies.

Use this structure:

```text
Her reaction is unfinished: [eyes move first / smile just begins / head starts turning / hand freezes halfway / body still continues the original task].
```

Good reaction examples:

- eyes brighten before a full smile
- smile just begins but is not complete
- brief glance toward the camera then away
- half-turn over one shoulder
- mouth slightly open as if about to answer
- eyebrows lift in recognition
- hand freezes halfway through action
- body continues walking while face turns back
- phone lowers a few centimeters
- shoulders remain facing the original task

### Rule 4 — Keep body and face out of sync

A real reaction happens in sequence. The eyes may notice first while the hand, shoulders, hips, or feet are still doing the original task.

Prompt this directly:

```text
Her eyes notice first, her head starts to turn, but her shoulders and hands are still committed to the original action.
```

### Rule 5 — Leave task residue visible

Include evidence of what she was doing before the interruption.

Examples:

- phone still open in hand
- receipt between fingers
- umbrella half-folded
- bag zipper not closed
- cup lifted but not yet sipped
- hair caught between fingers
- one foot mid-step
- chair slightly pushed back
- drink condensation on table
- crossbody strap twisted from movement

### Rule 6 — Add environmental residue

A real interruption often leaves a small trace in the space, not only on her body. Add one ordinary environmental trace when possible:

- chair pushed back slightly
- bag left open
- receipt on the table
- drink ring or condensation mark
- umbrella dripping near the door
- lift button still lit
- door partly open
- café tray slightly off-center
- tissue packet pulled open
- phone cable half-out of the bag

### Rule 7 — Include ambient cause when useful

Small sounds or environmental changes can explain why she reacts:

- friend calls her name
- phone vibrates
- order number is called
- MTR door beep sounds
- elevator chime sounds
- taxi horn or scooter passes
- sudden gust of wind
- rain starts again
- door opens nearby

### Rule 8 — Avoid performance language

Do not use words that turn the image into a planned pose.

Avoid:

- posing
- model pose
- standing attractively
- elegant pose
- perfect smile
- looking into camera
- fashion editorial
- magazine style
- cinematic beauty shot
- influencer photo
- luxury lifestyle shoot

## Positive Examples

### Example 1 — Convenience Store Drink Choice

```text
A casual iPhone snapshot inside a convenience store. She was choosing a drink from the fridge when her friend called her name from behind. Her right hand is still on the fridge handle, her eyes notice first, and her smile is only just beginning. Her shoulders are still angled toward the fridge, not fully turned to pose. Drink shelves stay behind the glass, softly visible. The moment feels interrupted, not staged.
```

### Example 2 — Café Queue Number

```text
A natural friend-shot in a small café. She was checking a paper queue number while waiting for food. The staff calls a number off-frame, so she looks up halfway, mouth slightly open as if about to ask “is that ours?” One hand still holds the receipt, the other rests near her phone. Her smile is incomplete, her posture still belongs to the waiting moment. Slightly off-center smartphone framing, ordinary table clutter, no posed portrait feeling.
```

### Example 3 — Rainy Street Umbrella

```text
A casual rainy-night street snapshot. She was folding a wet umbrella and trying to put it into her tote bag when a friend said her name. Her hand is still caught in the umbrella strap, hair sticks lightly to her cheek from humidity, and she turns only her face back. Her body is still facing the direction she was walking. Street lights reflect on wet pavement, but the image feels like a real interrupted moment rather than a planned night portrait.
```

### Example 4 — MTR Strap

```text
A real-life iPhone photo inside the MTR. She was gripping the overhead strap as the train moved. The train jerks slightly, and she gives a quick amused glance toward her friend holding the phone. Her hand remains curled around the strap, shoulder slightly lifted from tension, body still balancing with the train. Her expression is between concentration and a small laugh. Not a model pose, not a clean portrait.
```

### Example 5 — Wind Fixing Hair

```text
A casual outdoor snapshot on a bright street. She was fixing hair after a gust of wind when the camera was raised unexpectedly. One hand is still halfway near her temple, a few strands cross her cheek, and her eyes glance toward the camera before her smile fully forms. Her torso is still angled away, bag strap slightly twisted. The photo catches the moment before she decides whether to pose.
```

### Example 6 — Friend Arrives

```text
A natural daytime street photo. She was waiting for a friend while checking her phone. Someone arrives from off-frame, so she lowers the phone a few centimeters and starts to look up. Her face relaxes into recognition, but the smile has not fully landed. Her body remains leaned slightly toward the wall from waiting, not arranged for the camera. The image feels like a social moment was caught.
```

### Example 7 — Takeaway Bag Check

```text
A casual Hong Kong takeaway counter snapshot. She was checking whether the paper bag on the counter was hers. The staff asks a question off-frame, and she pauses mid-reach. Her fingers hover near the bag handle, eyes shift toward the staff, mouth slightly open. Receipt remains in her other hand. The moment is practical and unfinished, not posed.
```

### Example 8 — Bench Rest

```text
A relaxed iPhone photo at an outdoor bench. She was sitting down and pushing her bag aside when her friend made a joke. One hand is still behind her supporting weight, the other is near the bag strap. Her laugh is just starting, shoulders not squared to camera, one foot not fully settled. The image captures a transition, not a completed sitting pose.
```

## Negative Examples

Avoid prompts like these:

```text
She stands beautifully against a wall, looking at the camera with a soft smile, hands in pockets, elegant pose.
```

Why it fails: no event, no interruption, no unfinished reaction. It reads as a photoshoot.

```text
She leans casually on a café wall with a drink, perfect composition, beautiful lighting, stylish street fashion.
```

Why it fails: “casual” is claimed but no actual task or social trigger exists. It becomes influencer photography.

```text
She walks down a wet street and looks back at the camera, cinematic lights, perfect pose, elegant expression.
```

Why it fails: the look-back is staged because no one called her, nothing interrupted her, and the body is arranged for beauty.

```text
She sits on a bench with legs stretched, looking at the sky, perfect relaxed pose.
```

Why it fails: completed relaxation pose with no event change. It feels directed.

```text
She holds a phone and smiles at camera.
```

Why it fails: the phone is a prop, not a task. There is no message, vibration, reply, or interruption.

```text
Candid photo, natural pose, real-life feeling, beautiful girl, perfect face.
```

Why it fails: it uses abstract labels instead of event logic. The model will create a generic posed “candid.”

## Failure Prevention Rules

- Do not use “candid” alone. Always specify what she was doing.
- Do not use “natural pose” alone. Replace it with a real task and unfinished reaction.
- Do not let the body fully face the camera unless the event explains it.
- Do not use a full smile if the camera just interrupted her. Use a smile beginning, fading, or not fully landed.
- Do not make both eyes, shoulders, hips, and feet align toward the camera at the same time.
- Do not make hands idle. Hands should still belong to the previous task.
- Do not place objects as props. Objects should be in use, just used, or about to be used.
- Do not use wall-leaning unless she is waiting, checking something, listening, or reacting to someone.
- Do not use looking-back or camera eye contact unless something caused it and task residue remains visible.
- Do not make the expression peak-perfect. Use before-peak or after-peak expressions.
- Do not over-clean the scene. Real captured moments keep small mess, task residue, and imperfect timing.
- Do not use studio, magazine, fashion editorial, luxury, glamour, or cinematic language for everyday moments.
- Do not center everything perfectly. A slight off-center frame helps, but only when paired with real event logic.
- Do not make hair, clothes, and posture too controlled. Environment and activity should leave traces.
- Do not describe attractiveness before the event. Start with place, atmosphere, activity, interruption, then reaction.

## Direct Prompt Insert Version

```text
Make this feel like a real moment interrupted by a camera, not a posed photoshoot.

She was already [normal life task] when [small interruption] happened. Her reaction is unfinished: [eyes notice first / smile just begins / head starts turning / hand freezes halfway / phone lowers slightly / body keeps doing the original task]. Her face and body are slightly out of sync: eyes and head begin to react, but shoulders, hands, hips, or feet still belong to the original action.

Keep task residue visible: [phone still open / receipt in fingers / umbrella half-folded / bag zipper open / cup not yet sipped / hair caught between fingers / one foot mid-step / strap twisted]. Keep environmental residue visible: [chair slightly moved / drink ring on table / bag open / door gap / umbrella dripping / lift button still lit / tissue packet pulled open]. The image should feel like someone happened to take this photo before she had time to pose.

Avoid performance: no model pose, no perfect smile, no fully squared shoulders, no symmetrical stance, no hands-in-pockets posing, no fashion editorial feeling, no studio lighting, no influencer wall-leaning unless a real task or interruption explains it, no camera eye contact unless task residue and interruption cause are visible.
```

## Quick Fill-In Formula

```text
[place + atmosphere]. She was [event/task] when [interruption]. Her reaction is [unfinished reaction]. Her body still shows [task residue]. The photo feels accidentally captured by a friend, slightly off-center, with ordinary-life timing and no completed pose.
```
