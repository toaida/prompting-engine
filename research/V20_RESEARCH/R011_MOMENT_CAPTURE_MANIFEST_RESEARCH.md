## Background

Recent production testing revealed a recurring issue: images can still feel posed even when environment realism, focus hierarchy, and physical interaction are improved. The picture may look technically believable, but it still reads like the subject was asked to stand there for a planned photoshoot.

R011 focuses only on moment capture: event logic, interruption logic, reaction logic, unfinished actions, transitional moments, and social interactions. It does not propose a new engine, character system, or camera system.

## Research Question

What makes a photograph feel like “a real moment interrupted by a camera” instead of “a person posing for a camera”?

## Research Areas Covered

- Japanese gravure off-shot photography
- Japanese photobooks
- Celebrity off-shot photography
- Friend-shot culture
- iPhone casual snapshots
- Xiaohongshu candid photography
- Disposable camera culture
- Everyday social photography

# Section A — Research Findings

## A1. Candid feeling comes from an interrupted task, not from an attractive pose

Across off-shot, photobook, friend-shot, iPhone snapshot, and disposable-camera cultures, the strongest candid images usually imply that the person was already doing something before the camera arrived. The photo feels real because the camera interrupts an ongoing event.

Useful prompt logic:

```text
She was already [doing task] when [small interruption] happened, so her face/body is caught between continuing the task and reacting to the interruption.
```

The key is not “candid” as an adjective. The key is a before/current/after chain:

```text
before = task already happening
current = camera/interruption catches her mid-transition
after = she will return to the task or respond to someone
```

## A2. “Almost” is more believable than “complete”

A completed smile, completed pose, completed turn, completed hair adjustment, or completed drink sip feels arranged. A moment that is not yet finished feels captured.

Better states:

- smile just beginning
- turning but shoulders not yet aligned
- hand halfway to hair
- phone lowering but not pocketed
- drink lifted but not sipped
- bag zipper half-open
- umbrella partly folded
- foot mid-step
- sentence interrupted

## A3. Off-frame social cause matters

A candid photo needs a reason for the reaction. If she looks back, why? If she smiles, at what? If she pauses, what changed?

Common causes:

- friend says her name
- phone vibrates
- queue number is called
- door opens
- food arrives
- taxi pulls up
- MTR doors beep
- someone asks to pass
- wind changes hair
- rain starts again

## A4. The body should not finish the same reaction at the same time as the face

Real reactions cascade. Eyes move first, then head, then shoulder, then torso. If the whole body faces camera perfectly, it feels posed. If the face notices the camera while the body is still doing the original task, it feels captured.

Useful prompt language:

```text
her eyes notice first, her head starts to turn, but her shoulders and hands are still committed to the original action
```

## A5. Social snapshots contain “task residue”

A real moment often includes physical evidence of the interrupted task:

- phone still open in hand
- receipt between fingers
- umbrella half-folded
- bag zipper not closed
- cup still near lips
- hair caught between fingers
- one foot not fully planted
- chair pushed slightly back
- drink condensation on table
- crossbody strap twisted

Task residue prevents the image from becoming a portrait.

## A6. Friend-shot culture uses recognition, not performance

A friend-shot often captures recognition: she realizes someone she trusts is taking a photo. The expression is not a public model face. It may be amused, mildly annoyed, embarrassed, distracted, or warm. The camera is socially present but not commanding the whole body.

## A7. Disposable-camera and iPhone snapshots create permission for imperfection

The photo may have slight hand blur, off-center framing, edge crop, uneven flash, dim background, red-eye-adjacent brightness, or awkward timing. These flaws should support moment capture, not become random noise.

## A8. Xiaohongshu candid style often still hides planning; avoid influencer-candid traps

Many “candid” social images are actually posed: wall lean, looking away, coffee prop, walking pose, mirror check. R011 should borrow ordinary-life task logic, not influencer posing formulas.

## A9. Japanese photobook/off-shot realism often depends on liminal timing

Common photobook-feeling moments are before/after the obvious pose: after laughing, before speaking, between looking away and noticing camera, while adjusting hair after wind, while sitting down, while standing up, while reaching for an object. The emotional peak is less believable than the transition around it.

## A11. Environmental residue strengthens the interruption

The most robust captured moments show both body residue and environment residue. The space should remember the interruption: chair pushed back, receipt on table, umbrella dripping, drink ring, door gap, lift button still lit, or bag left open. This prevents the event from living only as text.

## A12. Ambient causes can replace obvious camera direction

A candid reaction does not always need “friend calls.” Ambient sound and environmental triggers are often stronger: MTR beeps, elevator chime, scooter passing, phone vibration, sudden wind, rain restarting, food arriving, queue number called. These create reaction without making the camera feel like director.

## A10. The final prompt must name event + interruption + reaction

A robust prompt should contain all three:

```text
Event: what she was doing
Interruption: what changed
Reaction: how her face/body begins to respond
```

If any one part is missing, posed feeling returns.

# PART 10: DEEPSEEK V4 PRO VERIFICATION
### Consolidation Date: 2026-06-02

## Verdict: **PASS WITH CONDITIONS**

R011 successfully addresses the core research question—how to reduce posed feeling and increase captured-moment feeling. The manifest is thorough, well-structured, and production-ready in its research phase. However, it requires minor tightening before full production deployment.

---

## Strengths

1. **Clear causal chain** — The Event → Interruption → Reaction framework is the strongest structural contribution. It directly attacks the posed feeling problem by forcing temporal logic into prompts.

2. **Task residue emphasis** — A5 correctly identifies that physical evidence of interrupted tasks (phone in hand, receipt between fingers, half-folded umbrella) is what separates candid moments from portraits.

3. **Cascading reaction logic** — A4's insight that "eyes move first, head follows later, shoulders remain committed to original task" is a precise, actionable pattern that current prompt systems lack.

4. **Failure pattern library** — Section E is comprehensive and directly usable as a negative prompt filter. The 50 patterns cover most common AI-generation pitfalls.

5. **No new systems proposed** — The manifest stays within prompt logic and does not suggest new engines, character systems, or camera systems. This is clean.

---

## Issues to Fix

### 1. Missing: Temporal specificity in prompt structure
The manifest says "before/current/after chain" but does not specify **how** to encode this in a prompt string. Current prompt systems often flatten temporal logic. Recommend adding a concrete prompt template:

```
[Event: {task description}] when [Interruption: {small change}] causes [Reaction: {partial, cascading response}]. Task residue visible: {one physical trace}.
```

### 2. Overlap between pattern libraries
Some events in Section B overlap with interruptions in Section C (e.g., B35 "putting phone back into bag" vs C2 "phone vibrates in her hand"). This is not fatal but could cause confusion during prompt assembly. Recommend a cross-reference note or deduplication.

### 3. Missing: Environmental continuity logic
The manifest focuses on subject behavior but does not address **environmental evidence of interruption**. For example:
- A chair pushed back slightly
- A napkin dropped
- A drink ring on the table
- A bag left open

These are the spatial equivalent of task residue. Consider adding a brief subsection in Section A or a small pattern library.

### 4. Failure pattern 44 is too absolute
"Looking at camera while supposedly busy" is listed as a failure pattern. However, real candid photos sometimes capture a subject looking at the camera while still holding task residue (e.g., phone still in hand, drink still near mouth). This pattern should be softened to "looking at camera with no task residue or interruption cause."

### 5. Missing: Audio/ambient context
The manifest does not address how ambient sound or silence affects candid feeling. In real photography, the sound of a camera shutter, a friend's voice, or street noise contributes to the "interrupted moment" feeling. Consider a brief note on this.

---

## Missing Patterns

### 1. **Group dynamics**
The manifest focuses on single-subject candid moments. Real candid photography often involves two or more people reacting to each other. Consider adding:
- A friend reaching into frame
- Two people reacting to the same interruption differently
- One person mid-laugh while another looks at camera

### 2. **Self-interruption**
Not all interruptions come from external sources. Sometimes the subject interrupts themselves:
- Forgets what they were about to say
- Changes mind about an action
- Realizes they forgot something
- Gets distracted by their own phone

### 3. **Environmental interruption without social cause**
Some of the best candid moments come from non-social interruptions:
- A sudden gust of wind
- A bird flying past
- A car horn
- A phone notification from the subject's own device

The current interruption library is heavily social (friend calls, waiter calls, door opens). Consider adding 5-10 non-social interruptions.

### 4. **Post-interruption return**
The manifest captures the moment of interruption but does not address what happens immediately after. A subject who has just been interrupted and is about to return to their task has a different quality than one who is still in the interruption. Consider adding "returning to task" as a reaction pattern.
